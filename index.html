        Liner notes

        Method

        The jukebox never invents a note. Every sound traces back to a number measured from a real NASA scene, printed in the table below. What this page adds is judgement: which measurement drives which musical parameter, and the constraints that keep eighteen bars of satellite data listenable as music.

        1 · Fetch

        Each record is a published NASA animation. Eighteen scenes are cut from it, evenly spaced, skipping title cards and fades, and encoded into one small clip that plays offline. The scene you are looking at is the instant the bar beside it was measured from, so the picture keeps moving inside the music instead of cutting between stills. The live tuner instead requests one WMS render per date from NASA GIBS.

        2 · Measure

        Every second pixel is read straight out of the canvas: luminance, saturation, a circular mean hue, Hasler–Süsstrunk colourfulness, position along the record's own colour bar, gradient texture via a Sobel pass, and sixteen vertical columns plus six horizontal bands of activity. Around 230,000 samples per frame, in about eight milliseconds.

        3 · Calibrate

        Space is black. A night-lights frame and an aerosol frame both average under ten percent luminance, so absolute readings would trap every record in the bottom octave. Each feature is therefore stretched between its own 10th and 90th percentile within the side, and the raw value is kept for display.

        4 · Compose

        Measurements become one bar: a drone, a chord, a bass line, sixteen melodic steps and percussion. Three constraints keep eighteen bars of data listenable: every pitch is drawn from a fixed mode, so no measurement can produce a wrong note; tempo moves at most seven BPM per bar; and the chord walks toward the degree the colour bar asks for one scale step at a time, so harmony is a progression rather than a series of jumps.

        5 · Play

        A Web Audio lookahead scheduler places events about 120 ms ahead of the clock, so the pulse stays steady while the page animates. Each bar plays its own scene, slowed to the length of the bar, so the maps drift rather than jump. The strip under the stage is an analyser on the master bus — what it shows is the audio graph, not a simulation — and the readout beneath it is the live measurement of the moving frame. The export path renders the same graph through an OfflineAudioContext: the WAV is the performance, not an approximation of it.
